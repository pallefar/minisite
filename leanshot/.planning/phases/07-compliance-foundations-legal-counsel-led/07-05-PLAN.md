# FTC HBNR Incident-Response Runbook

**Purpose.** When (not if) LeanShot discovers a breach of security under the FTC's Health Breach Notification Rule (16 CFR Part 318, as amended effective 2024-07-29), this runbook is the authoritative procedure. The 60-day notification clock is unforgiving. Following this runbook is how a sole-founder operation hits the deadline. Per D-01, no outside counsel is on retainer; the founder executes this runbook directly.

**When in doubt, notify.** The HBNR's expanded 2024 breach definition includes unauthorized *disclosure*, not just unauthorized acquisition. If you are uncertain whether an event qualifies, default to notifying. Over-notification is an annoyance; under-notification is a federal enforcement action.

## Definitions

(Define each term inline with a citation to 16 CFR Part 318 §318.2 where applicable. Required terms — write at least one paragraph per term:)

- **Breach of security** — unauthorized acquisition OR unauthorized disclosure of PHR identifiable health information held by a vendor of personal health records. (16 CFR §318.2(a); 2024 amendment expanded to include disclosure.)
- **PHR identifiable health information** — identifiable health information about an individual, drawn from multiple sources, that LeanShot holds or processes. For LeanShot, this is the 9 sync tables (injections, weights, meals, water, foodNoise, workouts, steps, supplements, mood, sleep, nsvs, photos, vials, costs, symptoms, settings) plus AI conversation history. (Enumerate explicitly — Phase 7 D-04 anchors this list.)
- **Secured vs unsecured PHR** — "secured" means encrypted per HHS guidance such that the data is unusable to unauthorized persons. Supabase Storage encryption-at-rest + TLS-in-transit alone do NOT make data "secured" for HBNR purposes — encryption keys are co-resident with the data. Treat all LeanShot PHR as **unsecured** unless and until the upgrade phase under CONTEXT D-02 ships envelope encryption with off-platform key custody.
- **500-record threshold** — breaches involving 500 or more individuals trigger contemporaneous FTC notification AND prominent media notice in the affected state(s); below 500, FTC is notified annually within 60 days of year-end (16 CFR §318.5(c)).
- **Discovery** — a breach is "discovered" the day LeanShot (i.e., the founder, an automated alert, or a third party reporting to support) first knows OR by exercising reasonable diligence would have known of the incident. The 60-day clock starts on the **calendar date of discovery**, not the date the breach occurred.

## 60-day notification clock

(Write at least 2 paragraphs covering:)

- **Clock start:** 16 CFR §318.4 — runs from the date of *discovery*, not occurrence. Document the discovery timestamp the moment it's known (a commit to this repo or a timestamped note in `.planning/decisions/` is acceptable evidence).
- **Clock duration:** 60 calendar days. Not business days. Not weekdays. **60 calendar days** — include weekends and holidays.
- **What must happen by day 60:** (1) every affected individual receives written notice (electronic notice is permitted post-2024); (2) FTC receives notice via the Notice of Breach of Health Information form linked below; (3) if ≥500 affected individuals in any one state, prominent media notice in that state.
- **FTC notification URL (verified at runbook authoring):** https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-ftcs-health-breach-notification-rule-0 — the "Notice of Breach" form is the official submission channel. If the URL is dead at incident time, search "FTC Notice of Breach of Health Information form" — do NOT skip notification because a URL changed.
- **Day-by-day target cadence (suggested, not statutory):** D+0 contain → D+7 root-cause analysis complete → D+30 individual notices drafted and reviewed → D+45 individual notices sent → D+50 FTC form submitted → D+55 media notice sent if ≥500 → D+60 hard deadline, all notifications dispatched.

## Breach decision tree

(Render an actual decision tree in Markdown — either an ASCII tree or a numbered flowchart. Required branches:)

1. **Is the event a "breach of security" under 16 CFR §318.2?**
   - Is PHR identifiable health information involved? (If no → not in HBNR scope; document and stop. If yes → continue.)
   - Was there unauthorized acquisition OR unauthorized disclosure? (If neither → not a breach; document and stop. If either → continue.)
2. **Severity matrix — how many individuals affected?**
   - **0 individuals confirmed but credible exposure (e.g., publicly-leaked credentials):** treat as breach; notify all potentially-affected individuals; FTC annual roll-up.
   - **1-499 individuals:** individual notice within 60 days; FTC annual roll-up within 60 days of calendar year end.
   - **≥500 individuals:** individual notice within 60 days AND contemporaneous FTC notice AND prominent media notice in each affected state.
3. **Default to notify.** If branch resolution is ambiguous, treat as a notifiable breach. Document the rationale for the chosen branch in the incident's post-incident review (below). **When in doubt, notify.** A repeated phrase intentional — it is the operative principle when one person is making the call alone.

(Document the v1 on-call posture explicitly, including the bus-factor accepted-risk per researcher Open Question #6:)

- **Primary on-call:** the founder. There is no secondary. This is the accepted bus-factor risk recorded in `.planning/decisions/COMPL-03-ACKNOWLEDGEMENT.md`.
- **Escalation sequence (sole-founder degenerate case):**
  1. Founder validates the event meets the breach-of-security definition (definitions section above).
  2. Founder walks the breach decision tree (above) and writes the resolution to a new file `.planning/incidents/YYYY-MM-DD-<short-slug>.md` with the discovery timestamp pinned.
  3. Founder runs the 60-day clock cadence above.
  4. **If accepted-risk D-01 (no counsel) flips during a real incident** — i.e., the breach is large, complex, or has litigation exposure that exceeds the founder's risk tolerance — engage outside counsel before day 60. - **Out-of-hours:** the 60-day clock does not pause for weekends, holidays, or off-hours. The founder commits to checking for HBNR-trigger signals (Sentry alerts, Supabase abuse flags, user-reported support emails) at least once every 24 hours during normal operating periods.
